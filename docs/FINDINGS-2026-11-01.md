# What changes when you swap a model: findings from 1 Nov 2026

Everything here was preregistered (a hash of the analysis plan was published before any output existed) and uses public
intent-classification data. The key numbers (the GLM-5.3-Flash format-hazard counts and test in section 1, and every
trigger-set number in section 2) were recomputed on a second machine by an agent that wrote its own code; the
section 2 base rate comes from a separate preregistered prevalence study; the other table cells and section 3 come from
migration-check's own reports. Each claim gives the command to rerun it and what it cost.

## 1. Three real upgrades, three different outcomes

Each pair was served on Together AI. We used three public datasets: Banking77 (770 items, CC BY 4.0), HWU64 (620,
CC BY 4.0) and CLINC150 (750, CC BY 3.0). The full label list went in every prompt, 20 items per call, 5 draws per
model.

| Upgrade                                  | Banking77    | HWU64        | CLINC150     | What it means                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| DeepSeek V4.1 Flash to its 1017 snapshot | 82.3 to 83.0 | 86.6 to 87.5 | 92.1 to 93.3 | No regression detected in any tested dataset; 2 CLINC150 intents better           |
| GLM-5.2 to GLM-5.3                       | 81.8 to 81.4 | 86.9 to 86.5 | 92.4 to 92.7 | Flat averages, but 3 Banking77 intents worse beyond run-to-run noise and 1 better |
| GLM-5.3 to GLM-5.3-Flash (cheaper tier)  | 80.6 to 82.6 | 86.5 to 81.2 | 92.7 to 92.6 | A format break on HWU64; an improvement on Banking77                              |

Accuracy is strict accuracy in percent: an answer must be an allowed label, spelled exactly. Intents marked worse or
better pass migration-check's graded test, which is family-wise corrected across all intents of a dataset by a
single-step min-p permutation test.

**The cheaper tier.**

- On HWU64, GLM-5.3-Flash rewrote multi-word labels into snake_case ("datetime query" became "datetime_query") in 11 of 155 calls, against 0 of 155 for GLM-5.3 (Fisher exact p = 0.0008).
- All 220 off-list answers sit in those 11 calls: when it slips, the whole batch slips.
- Among answers that do follow the label list, Flash is 87.4% accurate (2,517 of 2,880) against 86.5% for GLM-5.3. That difference is descriptive, with a paired 95% interval of -0.6 to +2.5 points.
- So the 5.3-point drop is mostly an output-format change: among answers that follow the format, no loss of task accuracy is detected. That subset is selected (inputs Flash answers off-list leave its denominator), and the drop would still break any parser that expects the allowed labels.
- On Banking77 the same switch helped. Only a per-task, repeated replay tells you which.

**Exploratory follow-up (not preregistered): normalize the labels.** If a team lowercases answers and maps underscores
and hyphens to spaces before comparing (applied to both the answer and the allowed label), the cheaper tier is not
detectably worse on any dataset. The comparison counts every call, and a missing answer counts as wrong:

| Dataset | GLM-5.3 | GLM-5.3-Flash | Difference, 95% interval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HWU64 | 2,680 of 3,100 (86.5%) | 2,706 of 3,100 (87.3%) | +0.8 [-0.6, +2.3] |
| Banking77 | 3,105 of 3,850 (80.6%) | 3,183 of 3,850 (82.7%) | +2.0 [+0.7, +3.4] |
| CLINC150 | 3,476 of 3,750 (92.7%) | 3,473 of 3,750 (92.6%) | -0.1 [-1.1, +1.0] |

189 of Flash's 220 off-list HWU64 answers are correct once normalized. The HWU64 counts were recomputed independently on
a second machine. Together listed GLM-5.3-Flash at $0.15 input and $0.50 output per million tokens, against $1.40 and
$4.40 for GLM-5.3, when we read the pricing page on 1 Nov 2026 (23:24 UTC). On these replays the cheaper tier cost
$0.71 against $8.10 for the same calls (11.4 times less), retries included; integration cost not counted. Per dataset:
HWU64 $0.23 against $2.38, Banking77 $0.35 against $4.28, CLINC150 $0.13 against $1.43. These are the token counts each
response reported, priced at those list rates with no discount for cached input; 15 attempts that timed out reported
no usage and are not counted (13 of them GLM-5.3). The ratio is above the roughly 9x list-price ratio because Flash
wrote 24% fewer output tokens (1.31 million against 1.72 million) and needed fewer retries. So the decision this replay supports is: the cheaper
tier, plus a one-line label normalization, for these three tasks. Without the replay, the 5.3-point drop would have
looked like a reason not to switch.

Rerun one pair (set `MIGRATION_CHECK_BASE_URL=https://api.together.xyz/v1` and `MIGRATION_CHECK_API_KEY` first):

```bash
migration-check run --inputs hwu64_inputs.jsonl --labels hwu64_labels.txt \
  --old openai-compatible:zai-org/GLM-5.3 --new openai-compatible:zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash \
  --draws 5 --batch 20 --out runs-glm-flash-hwu64
```

The inputs file holds one `{"id", "input", "gold"}` line per HWU64 test item. Cost: $29.43 on Together for all nine
pair-dataset runs (output tokens dominate, because these models reason before answering).

Two pairs we had preregistered were dropped before any measurement: Qwen3.6-Plus vs Qwen3.7-Plus, and Qwen3.5-9B vs
Qwen3.8-Flash. Together routes those models to a third-party provider, which would have required turning on
organization-wide data sharing. The replacements were chosen only from models Together serves itself.

## 2. A behaviour change in Claude Sonnet 5.5

Base rate first. In a preregistered prevalence study, Claude Sonnet 5.5 returned two or more JSON answer objects in 5 of
216 first-attempt calls (2.3%, Wilson 95% interval 1.0% to 5.3%; 3 of 108 batches affected), against 0 of 216 for
Claude Sonnet 4.5. The calls used migration-check v0.4.1's default prompt at up to 20 messages per call on public data
(Banking77, HWU64 and CLINC150, 2 draws per batch), through the Claude Code CLI at its defaults (no extended thinking).
An independent detector agreed with migration-check on all 432 first attempts. This is a rate on public benchmark data
in one configuration, not a production rate.

A searched trigger set makes the behaviour reproducible. 6 public CLINC150 messages, narrowed from a batch that failed
repeatedly (the same batch failed in both of its draws in the prevalence study), make Sonnet 5.5 answer, write "Wait",
and answer again in 17 of 20 calls, against 0 of 20 for matched messages and 0 of 20 for Sonnet 4.5. That is the rate
on the searched set, not on traffic. See
[examples/sonnet55-self-correction](../examples/sonnet55-self-correction/README.md) for the scope, the fixes we
measured, and the one-command rerun.

## 3. A null, reported: checkpoint selection on an easy fine-tune

We asked how often picking a fine-tuning checkpoint from one eval run picks a significantly worse checkpoint.

- Setup: a Qwen3.5-9B LoRA on CLINC150, 8 checkpoints, 10 draws each on held-out selection and confirmation halves.
- Result: accuracy rose steadily (95.2% to 96.9% on the confirmation half). Every single-draw pick landed within noise of the best, so the share of wrong picks was 0 of 10 and one draw was enough.
- On a monotone, easy fine-tune, single-run selection was fine. The risk we measured is in vendor migrations, not here.

## Limits

- Intent classification and batched JSON outputs only. Free text is not scored beyond exact match.
- 5 or 10 draws per model; vendor models can change behind the same name after these dates.
- No outside team has used this on its own traffic yet.
